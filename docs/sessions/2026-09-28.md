## Why the oracle fixtures are independent of the implementation

1. **They come from equations written before the code.** `MODEL.md` states the model as
   equations E1–E15, and each fixture carries its derivation step by step, citing them.
   The expected numbers were worked out by hand for tiny, hand-built flocks (two to five
   birds, one rule at a time) and evaluated case by case in a Python calculator, straight
   from those formulas. No TypeScript implementation existed when the fixtures were
   written, and no fixture value was ever produced by running it.
2. **The PRNG vectors come from outside the repo's code.** They were computed with a
   Python transcription of the public-domain mulberry32 C reference. They were then
   cross-checked against the separate generator in the prototype, and all four seeds
   match.
3. **Most oracles need no implementation numbers at all.** Besides exact one-step
   arithmetic, they use:
   - a conservation law (alignment conserves total velocity in a mutually visible flock);
   - a limiting case (with every rule off, agreement is frozen bit for bit);
   - a derived convergence bound (alignment alone in a well-mixed flock decays
     deviations by (1 − k)^t, giving agreement above 0.99 by step 600);
   - the equations' symmetries (diagonal reflection bit for bit, permutation,
     translation);
   - replay.
4. **The harness is separate.** `flock.oracles.test.ts` drives only the public API
   (`init`, `step`, `measure`), validates every fixture against its own strict schema,
   and does its comparisons with its own torus arithmetic. It imports nothing from
   `src/` beyond the public entry point.
5. **Thresholds were fixed before the first run and never adjusted.**
   - The brief's example "alignment 0 ⇒ agreement stays low" cannot be settled by the
     equations while separation and cohesion are on, because attraction and repulsion
     alone can align some flocks. So the derived oracle is its exact form: all rules
     off, agreement frozen.
   - The default-rules version is kept, labelled `expected-qualitative`, and its failure
     would be a finding to investigate, not a threshold to tune.
6. **They catch bugs.** Twelve bugs were planted one at a time, and every one was caught
   by at least one test:
   - the alignment mean including the bird itself;
   - no shortest-way-round displacement;
   - the acceleration cap removed;
   - separation weighted by 1/d instead of 1/d²;
   - a vx/vy typo in cohesion;
   - the last beacon used instead of the nearest;
   - `step` writing into its input;
   - a cluster link test of ≤ instead of <;
   - the speed floor removed;
   - an unseeded heading draw;
   - the obstacle push with the wrong sign;
   - a changed mulberry32 constant.

   The source was restored after each one and the suite re-run green.

Measured margins, recorded but not tuned: the starting agreement of 72 birds is 0.07–0.18
(threshold below 0.4); alignment alone reaches 1.0000 at step 600 (threshold above 0.99);
without alignment, agreement at step 1200 is 0.07–0.36 (threshold below 0.5). A step of
72 birds costs about 22 µs.

## Decisions and deviations from the brief

- **TypeScript 6.0.3, not 7.** typescript-eslint supports only TypeScript below 6.1.
- **Companion dev packages** were approved by the owner in this session (listed in
  ADR-0001).
- **`schemaVersion` literals** (`sourcemanifest/1`, `runrecord/1`) on the documents
  persisted on their own, to satisfy "version every schema". Embedded schemas are
  versioned through the documents that contain them.
- **Stricter than the brief's sketch:**
  - ids are plain identifiers;
  - digests are lowercase-hex SHA-256;
  - the seed is an unsigned 32-bit integer;
  - an Action's range must be ordered and contain its default;
  - events must be ordered by step;
  - `initialState` is required and non-null;
  - a primitive version is an exact major.minor.patch;
  - a primitive id is dotted camelCase.
- **Extended primitive interface.** It adds `fixedTimestep` and typed observables.
  `describe()` also reports each variable's range, default, phase (init or live) and
  whether it is a whole number, and describes each observable.
- **Observable definitions, which the brief left open.** `meanNeighbourDistance` is the
  mean nearest-neighbour distance. `clusterCount` links birds at a fixed 4.5, so that it
  compares fairly across perception settings.
- **World shape.** The model uses a torus, as the brief says. The prototype's bounded
  island is kept as the visual direction only.
- **The gate checks requirement evidence** (`[Rnn]` tags) as well as script exit codes,
  so it cannot pass before the gate's own tests exist.
- **Parameter names and defaults** follow the prototype (separation 1.2, alignment 1.0,
  cohesion 0.8, perception 4.5, 72 birds), recorded as teaching choices.
